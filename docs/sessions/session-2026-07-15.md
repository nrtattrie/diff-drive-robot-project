  - No strong objection for motor, driver, buck-converter, and general Arduino testing at its roughly $45 price.
  - Treat it as an inexpensive hobby switching supply, not calibrated laboratory equipment.
  - Test voltage accuracy, CC behavior, output switching, loaded stability, fan, and heating during the return period; user reports include display error, RF noise, and occasional defective CC behavior.
  - Do not use its 5 V/2 A USB port for the Raspberry Pi 5.
- Explained the safe multimeter procedure for adjusting/verifying the supply:
  - meter leads in `COM` and `V/ohm`, meter set to DC voltage above the expected range;
  - connect across supply positive/negative, enable output, and adjust while watching the meter;
  - disable output before changing connections;
  - never place a multimeter in current mode directly across the supply terminals because that creates a short.
- Defined a layered Pi protection approach:
  - keep the Pi on its official 27 W USB-C supply during early bench work;
  - test the bench supply and DROK only with a dummy load before connecting the Pi;
  - a fuse protects wiring from sustained excessive current, a capacitor reduces dips/noise, and neither protects against accidentally applying excessive voltage;
  - use an eFuse/overvoltage-disconnect device or protected Pi power HAT for actual overvoltage protection;
  - a `TPS25947`-class eFuse supports adjustable overvoltage cutoff, roughly 0.5-6 A current limiting, short-circuit protection, soft start, thermal protection, and reverse-current blocking.
- Proposed preliminary protection values to refine after measuring current and choosing wire gauge:
  - 10 A automotive blade fuse for the main system feed;
  - 5 A automotive blade fuse on the 12 V input branch feeding the Pi regulator;
  - 470-1000 uF, 25 V, low-ESR, 105 C electrolytic at each Pololu motor driver's `VIN`/`GND`, physically close to the driver;
  - no arbitrary additional capacitor on the Pi 5 V rail until the regulator/eFuse requirements are known.
- Defined DROK validation before Pi connection: adjust to 5.10 V with no Pi, power-cycle repeatedly, load-test progressively, verify voltage at the end of the actual cable, never adjust while the Pi is connected, and lock/cover the adjustment after calibration.
